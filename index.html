Must See · Morning
Gyeongbokgung Palace & Bukchon Hanok Village
Gyeongbokgung: arrive before 9am — changing of the guard ceremony at 10am and 14:00 (free, spectacular). The palace grounds cover 410,000m² with restored throne halls, gardens and pavilions. Allow 2 hours · Bukchon: the upper village (north of Anguk station) has the best-preserved hanok lanes — 30-minute walk from Gyeongbokgung. Go before 10am before tour groups arrive. The lower village near Samcheong-dong is more café-lined but equally charming
🥢
Food & Culture
Gwangjang Market · Seoul's oldest covered market
The food stalls here are legendary — bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes, crispy, served with makgeolli), yukhoe (raw beef tartare with egg yolk and Asian pear), mayak gimbap (bite-sized seaweed rice rolls the size of your thumb, addictive). Sit at the stall counters, not the restaurant section. Open until late. The textile market surrounds it for fabric and traditional crafts
🛍️
Shopping & Street Food
Myeongdong & Insadong
Myeongdong: Korea's K-beauty capital — Innisfree, Etude House, Laneige, Missha all have flagship stores. The street food vendors at night are exceptional: egg bread, hotteok, tornado potatoes, corn dogs. Very tourist-heavy but fun · Insadong: artisan craft shops, traditional tea houses, hanji (Korean paper) goods. More atmosphere, less commercial than Myeongdong · Ikseon-dong alley (5 min walk from Insadong): converted early 20th century Korean houses now housing tiny cocktail bars and coffee shops — the coolest evening drinking spot in Seoul
🎧
Nightlife · Seoul
Clubbing — Itaewon is your area
Deep house / tech house: Soap Seoul — Seoul's premier underground club, powerful sound system, international DJs, tech-house and deep house. Paper — rooftop club with deep house and panoramic city views, Seoul's only rooftop club · Hip hop / funk: Cakeshop — iconic underground venue, hosted Peggy Gou, hip hop and electronic, warehouse feel. Lucidream — laid-back, hip hop and R&B, foreigner-friendly. Owl Lounge — deep house, nu disco, tech house and hip hop all in one · Cover ₩10,000–20,000 · Last subway before midnight — plan taxi or stay till 5am first train · Good nights: 3 or 4 July (no early starts)
🌇
Evening
Namsan Seoul Tower at dusk
Take the cable car from Myeongdong (5 min walk) — departs every few minutes, ¥9,000 return. The observation deck at 237m gives 360° views of the entire Seoul basin. Go 30 minutes before sunset — watch the city transform from day to night. The love locks on the fence are iconic. Alternatively hike up through Namsan Park (40 min, beautiful forested path) and take cable car back down. Best photo spot is the terrace on the south side for the Han River in the background

Neighbourhood · Morning
Yanaka · Old Tokyo preserved
One of the few Tokyo neighbourhoods that survived both the 1923 earthquake and WWII bombing — feels like Tokyo 100 years ago. Walk Yanaka Ginza shopping street (covered arcade selling traditional snacks, crafts, cat-themed everything). The Yanaka Cemetery is genuinely lovely for a stroll — cherry trees, old stone graves, cats everywhere. Best visited early morning before it gets hot. Start at Nippori station and wander south toward Nezu shrine (one of Tokyo's oldest, vermillion torii gates, no crowds)
🐟
Food · Morning
Tsukiji Outer Market · Tokyo's greatest breakfast
The inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu in 2018, but the outer market (where all the food stalls are) is very much alive. Go 7:30–9:00am before tour groups arrive. Must-eat: fresh tuna rice bowl (maguro don) at a counter stall — look for queues of Japanese people, not foreigners. Tamagoyaki (rectangular sweet egg omelette) on a stick. Uni (sea urchin) if you haven't had enough in Hokkaido. Oysters. Fresh sushi at 8am is genuinely one of life's great experiences. Budget ¥1,500–3,000pp for a proper breakfast feast
🎋
Neighbourhood
Harajuku · Two very different streets
Takeshita-dori: covered pedestrian street of wild teen fashion, crepes, overloaded bubble tea. Chaotic, fun, completely unlike anywhere else. Best after 11am when it gets busy · Omotesando (2 min walk from Takeshita): the exact opposite — wide tree-lined boulevard of high-end international flagships (Prada, Louis Vuitton, Comme des Garçons) designed by Tadao Ando and other architects. The buildings themselves are worth seeing. The side streets (Ura-Harajuku) between these two worlds have the best independent fashion and coffee
⛩️
Morning · Best early
Meiji Shrine · Japan's most important Shinto shrine
Dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken. The forested approach (700m of gravel path lined with 120,000 trees) is serene and one of the great walks in Tokyo. Arrive before 8am to see morning prayers and occasionally traditional wedding ceremonies (Saturdays especially). Free entry. The iris garden nearby is beautiful in July. Takes 45 min to do properly
🍶
Evening
Shinjuku — Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho
Golden Gai: 200+ tiny bars each fitting around 6 people — pick a bar by peering in the window, pay a small cover charge, talk to whoever is there. Omoide Yokocho: smoke-filled yakitori alley under the Shinjuku tracks, beer and grilled chicken, unchanged since the 1950s. Both within 5 min walk of Shinjuku Station east exit
🎧
Nightlife · Tokyo · Best nights 6 or 7 Jul
Tokyo Clubbing · Shibuya & Shinjuku
Deep/Progressive House: Contact (Shibuya) — hidden under a Dogenzaka parking lot, the most respected underground club in Tokyo. World-class sound, intimate, serious music crowd. Check lineup in advance at ra.co. Fri/Sat ¥3,000–4,000 · VENT (Aoyama) — only real club in the Aoyama area, hi-res sound system, consistent deep house and minimal techno programming. 2 min from Omotesando station. Fri/Sat 11pm–5am · WOMB (Shibuya) — Tokyo institution since 2000, Asia's largest mirror ball, four floors, powerful sound. More commercial than Contact/VENT but consistently strong international bookings · Hip Hop/Funk: Zero Tokyo (Shinjuku/Kabukicho Tower) — multiple floors including hip hop nights, newest major Tokyo club. Worth checking lineup · DJ Bar Koara (Shibuya backstreets) — tiny basement bar, Techno/House/Disco/Funk/Soul, cheap entry (¥1,000), excellent DJs, neighbourhood favourite · Note: 8 Jul is a 14:35 flight so avoid a big night on 7 Jul — or keep it to Contact/VENT which wind down by 5am

Must Do · Cape Kamui
Cape Kamui · The tip of Shakotan
Walk from the trailhead to the cape tip is 20 minutes each way — moderate but exposed. The rock at the very end gives 270° views of the Japan Sea with the Shakotan Blue water below. Sea caves visible from the clifftop. In July the wild flowers are out. Go early morning before tour buses arrive. The colour of the water is genuinely unlike anything in mainland Japan — iridescent turquoise caused by the depth and clarity · There's a small shrine at the cape tip where fishermen pray before going out to sea
🏊
Swimming
Shimamui Beach · Best beach on the peninsula
A 20-minute drive from Cape Kamui — rocky arch frames a small beach with crystal clear water. July sea temperature is around 20°C — cold but swimmable. The coastal rock formations are spectacular. Much less visited than the cape itself. Bring snorkelling gear if you have it — visibility is extraordinary
🚗
Drive Tips
Route 229 · Shakotan Peninsula coastal road
The coastal road hugging the peninsula is one of Hokkaido's most beautiful drives. Stops worth making: Yobetsu fishing port (small, authentic, fishermen mending nets in the morning), Kamui Misaki Lighthouse viewpoint, the rock formations at Ogon-hama. Total lap of the peninsula takes about 2.5 hrs at a leisurely pace with stops. Petrol station in Bikuni — fill up there as there's nothing at the cape end

Flat island, easy driving, coral-clear water. Base in Hirara — central, walkable, local restaurants and harbour sunsets. Far more authentic than the resort strip on the west coast.

Miyakojima · 3 nights

🏖️
Beach Guide · Comparisons
Miyakojima's best beaches
Sunayama Beach: the iconic beach — a natural limestone arch frames the bay, water is impossible turquoise. Walk through a sand dune path to reach it. Gets busy by 10am. Best 7–9am for photography · Yonaha Maehama Beach: officially Japan's finest beach — 7km of white sand, shallow water stretches 100m out, looks Caribbean. Very photogenic. The water is warmer here as it's shallower · Painagama Beach: closest to Hirara town, calm sheltered bay, good snorkelling off the rocks at the south end, easy to combine with a Hirara lunch stop · Yoshino Beach: best snorkelling — coral reef starts just a few metres from shore, sea turtles, tropical fish. Small, no facilities. Bring water · Recommendation: Sunayama for photos/instagram, Yonaha Maehama for a full beach day, Yoshino for snorkelling
🌉
Drive
Irabu Bridge & Irabu Island
The 3.54km bridge opened in 2015 is Japan's longest toll-free bridge — ocean views in every direction. On Irabu Island itself: Toguchi no Hama beach (north shore, wild and remote), the old fishing village of Sawada, and Nakano Shima viewpoint looking back at the bridge. Allow 2 hours for the island loop. Fuel up in Hirara before crossing as there's only one small petrol station on Irabu
🏕️
Worth Visiting
Higashihennazaki Cape · Eastern tip of the island
A long narrow finger of coral rock extending into the sea — 30-minute walk each way with sea on both sides. Japanese lighthouse at the end. The most remote-feeling spot on the island. Combine with Yonaha Maehama Beach nearby for a perfect east-side half day
🍶
Evening · Hirara
Awamori bars & seafood izakayas
Awamori is Okinawa's distilled spirit — made from Thai indica rice, aged in clay pots, stronger and more complex than Japanese shochu. Try it on the rocks (mizuwari). The main Hirara bar streets are around Nishizato-dori — small izakayas serving fresh tuna, lobster, sea urchin alongside local Orion beer and awamori. Ask for the daily catch — whatever the fishermen brought in that morning. Budget ¥3,000–4,000pp for a proper evening including drinks

Dawn · Must Do
Okunoin Cemetery · Koyasan · At dawn or after dark
Japan's largest cemetery with over 200,000 tombstones beneath ancient cedar trees — some trees are 600 years old and 60m tall. A 2km stone-lantern-lit path leads to the Kobo Daishi mausoleum where the founder of Shingon Buddhism is said to be in eternal meditation. The lanterns are lit at all hours. Going at dawn (5:30–6:30am, before the morning ceremony) or after dark gives an experience unlike anything else in Japan. Major companies have elaborate stone monuments here — Toyota, Nissan, Panasonic. It's simultaneously moving, strange and beautiful
🏙️
Osaka
Dotonbori vs Shinsekai
Dotonbori: Osaka's famous canal strip with giant neon signs (the Glico running man, the giant mechanical crab). Street food paradise — takoyaki (octopus balls), kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers), ramen, fresh crab legs. Best after dark when the lights reflect in the canal. Very touristy but genuinely fun · Shinsekai (5 min south by subway): a completely different Osaka — a faded retro district built in 1912 modelled on Paris and New York, now a local working-class area with kushikatsu restaurants under the Tsutenkaku Tower. Feels like time travel. Try kushikatsu at a counter stall — the rule is never double-dip the shared sauce. Order by pointing at what you want
🎭
Kyoto · Evening
Pontocho & Gion
Pontocho: a single narrow alley running parallel to the Kamo River — Kyoto's most atmospheric dining lane. Traditional machiya restaurants on both sides, some with kawadoko (riverside terraces over the water) in summer. Book ahead or arrive early for the best spots. Walk the full length (10 min) before choosing · Gion: the famous geisha district — Hanamikoji street is the most atmospheric. Best chance of seeing a geiko or maiko heading to an engagement is 17:30–18:30 or after 21:30. Never pursue or photograph them without permission. The streets around Gion-shijo station are beautiful to walk at night even without seeing geiko
⛩️
Fushimi Inari · Key Tips
Fushimi Inari Taisha · The full experience
The famous 10,000 torii gates photo is taken about 10 minutes up the main path — but 95% of visitors turn back there. If you continue another 30 minutes you enter the upper mountain network where you'll be virtually alone even in July. The full circuit of Mount Inari takes 2–3 hours and the highest point (233m) has views over Kyoto and the Uji Plain. The torii gates don't end — there are side paths with gates stretching kilometres into the cedar forest. The moss-covered stone foxes (Inari's messenger) watching from altars along the path are extraordinary. Open 24 hours. Dawn truly transforms it
🎋
Arashiyama · Detail
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove & Tenryu-ji
The bamboo grove is 5 minutes from Saga-Arashiyama station — it's genuinely spectacular in person, the way the stalks block out the sky and creak in the wind. It's also genuinely very short (about 200m). Go before 8am. The best photos are from the north end of the path looking south into the bamboo tunnel · Tenryu-ji garden (adjacent) is Kyoto's finest Zen garden — ranked first among Kyoto's five great Zen temple gardens. The garden design borrowed the Arashiyama hills as its backdrop (shakkei technique). Worth the ¥1,000 entry. Hiroshima · Peace Memorial & Nakashima
▾
🕊️
Essential · Peace Memorial Museum
Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum
Allow 2–3 hours minimum. The museum is among the most important and moving in the world — not just about Hiroshima but about nuclear weapons, human cost, and responsibility. The personal belongings of victims — a child's lunchbox, a school uniform, a watch stopped at 8:15 — are devastating in a way photographs of destruction alone cannot be. Go without rushing. The east building opened in 2019 with a powerful new permanent exhibition. The A-Bomb Dome (Genbaku Dome) is 1 min walk — the only structure left standing at the hypocenter, now a UNESCO World Heritage site. Stand in front of it and look at the Peace Memorial Park behind. The Children's Peace Monument (inspired by Sadako Sasaki who folded 1,000 paper cranes hoping to be healed of radiation sickness) is just north of the museum
🍴
Hiroshima Speciality
Hiroshimayaki · Okonomimura
Hiroshima okonomiyaki is completely different to Osaka style — instead of mixing the ingredients together, they're layered: crepe base, cabbage, bean sprouts, pork belly, noodles (soba or udon), egg, and sweet Worcestershire sauce. More substantial, more complex. Okonomimura (OK World) is a five-story building entirely filled with okonomiyaki stalls — watch the cook make it on the iron griddle in front of you. Sit at the counter. Budget ¥1,000–1,200. The best versions include oysters (Hiroshima produces 70% of Japan's farmed oysters)

Island Life
Miyajima Island · Beyond the torii gate
Itsukushima Shrine: the orange torii gate in the water is one of Japan's three views — most photogenic at high tide (gate appears to float) but the mudflats at low tide allow you to walk to it. Check tide times before going · Mount Misen (535m): take the ropeway from Momijidani Park (two stages, ¥2,000 return) then 30-min hike to the summit. Views of the Seto Inland Sea and Hiroshima city are spectacular. On a clear July day you can see up to 50 islands · The deer are sacred (messengers of the gods) and entirely tame — they'll walk up to you and steal your map. Do not feed them. They particularly like paper · Daisho-in Temple: one of Shingon Buddhism's most sacred sites, at the base of Mount Misen. 500 stone lanterns, prayer wheels, extraordinary moss-covered guardian figures. Fewer crowds than the shrine · Oysters: Miyajima is famous for them — eat them grilled on charcoal at the waterfront stalls (¥250 each). Also try momiji manju (maple leaf-shaped cakes filled with red bean paste — the island's most famous snack)

Senso-ji Temple · Best before 7am
Tokyo's oldest temple, dedicated to Kannon the bodhisattva of mercy, founded in 628AD. The main gate (Kaminarimon, the Thunder Gate) with its 700kg lantern is one of Tokyo's most photographed sights.
